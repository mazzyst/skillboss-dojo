# Rewire the retro

**Hall:** Agile & Team Flow · **Blanks:** 3 · **Par:** 2m00s

Retros happen once a quarter, everyone vents, nothing changes. Make the loop actually loop.

## The artifact

Each run of underscores is one blank. Fill every blank with a token from the pool below; each token is used at most once.

```text
retrospective:
  cadence: "end of every ______"      # the inspect-and-adapt heartbeat
  prime_directive: "assume everyone did their best with what they knew"
  output: "one __________ improvement" # a commitment, not a feeling
  follow_up: "goes into the next sprint ______"
  # improvements compete with features — or they never happen
```

## The pool

`actionable` · `backlog` · `incident` · `optional` · `quarter` · `roadmap` · `sprint` · `standup`

<details>
<summary>Stuck? Hints (no answers)</summary>

1. The inspect-and-adapt loop beats at the same rhythm as delivery.
2. One concrete change someone can DO next week — not a mood.
3. Improvements must compete with features in the same prioritized list.

</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Solution and why it matters</strong> — spoilers</summary>

| # | Answer |
|---|---|
| 1 | `sprint` |
| 2 | `actionable` |
| 3 | `backlog` |

**Why it matters**

1. **`sprint`** — A retro per iteration keeps the feedback loop short enough to act on; quarterly retros audit ancient history nobody can fix.
2. **`actionable`** — The retro's contract is one concrete, ownable change; a list of vents with no owner is why "nothing changes".
3. **`backlog`** — Putting the improvement into the backlog forces a real prioritization decision; side-lists of "someday" actions quietly die.

</details>
