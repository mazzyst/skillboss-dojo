# SkillBoss envelope

Apache-2.0 · from the SkillBoss Dojo · see [LICENSE](LICENSE) and [NOTICE](NOTICE)

Your conversation with your agent becomes something other people can play.

Skills your own coding agent follows in your own project. Your agent
interviews you, drafts the content, and runs the guard on your machine.
You read it and say GO. Nothing of ours runs in your request path. No key,
no prompt and no conversation leaves your machine. No telemetry, in any
skill, ever.

## What is here today

Two skills. More arrive one at a time, each behind its own trial.

| Skill | What it does | Writes |
|---|---|---|
| [boss](boss/SKILL.md) | turns a moment your agent almost fooled you into a boss: a short scene, four shots, one right, a lesson, twenty seconds to play | one boss file, after you say "forge it" |
| [debrief](debrief/SKILL.md) | turns something that went wrong in your app into a blameless story: what happened, what worked, what you would do differently | one debrief file, after you say "file it" |

The format they write is [SPEC.md](SPEC.md): a draft until its ruling is
signed.

## The three tests every piece must pass

| Test | What makes it true |
|---|---|
| Shareable | one file, readable without an account, no claim word in it |
| Playable | twenty seconds, one shot, a lesson after |
| Useful | it ends on a door: a drill for the trap it names |

A piece that fails a test is not published in that form.

## How to use one

1. Read the skill yourself before your agent follows it. It is a few pages.
2. Tell your agent, in plain words: "forge a boss from this".
3. Answer its questions. Judge its wrong answers. Play the boss first.
4. Say "forge it". Keep the file.

## What a skill never does

It never claims your application is safe, correct or finished. A boss says
what happened to one person and what they learned. What it is worth to the
next player is their judgement, not ours.
